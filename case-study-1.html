Product Design

Atlas Banking App

Rebuilding a mobile banking experience around speed, clarity, and the quiet confidence people need when they’re moving money.

Role
Lead Product Designer
Timeline
4 months · 2024
Team
2 designers, 5 engineers

01 — Overview

A bank people liked, with a flow they didn’t.

Atlas is a digital-first bank with a loyal base of around 400,000 customers. People came for the friendly brand and stayed for the zero-fee accounts — but the core action, sending money, had grown into a seven-step maze of confirmations, warnings, and dead ends.

Support tickets told the story: transfers were the single biggest source of confusion, and drop-off spiked right at the moment of confirmation. The team had been adding safeguards one at a time, and each was sensible on its own. Together they had turned a 20-second task into a small act of courage.

02 — Problem

Friction had been mistaken for safety.

I started by mapping the existing transfer flow end to end and running moderated sessions with twelve customers. The pattern was consistent: people weren’t reassured by the extra steps, they were unsettled by them. Every interruption read as “something might be wrong.”

Every extra tap in a money flow is a moment for doubt to creep in.

That reframed the brief. The goal wasn’t to remove safeguards — it was to make trust the default and surface friction only when the risk was real. Safety and speed weren’t opposites; they’d just been wired together badly.

03 — Process

From seven steps to one screen.

I rebuilt the flow around a single, editable summary screen: amount, recipient, and timing all visible and changeable in place, with confirmation as a held button rather than a separate page. Risk checks moved to the background and only stepped forward for new payees or unusual amounts.

We prototyped three versions in Figma and tested each with the same twelve people. The winning direction wasn’t the prettiest — it was the one where no one asked “wait, did it go through?”

04 — Outcome

Half the time, a fraction of the doubt.

After a phased rollout to 100% of users over six weeks, median time-to-transfer dropped by 52% and transfer-related support tickets fell by just under a third. Most tellingly, completion rate at the confirmation step climbed from 81% to 96%.

The pattern we built — trust by default, friction on demand — became the template for how the rest of the app handles sensitive actions.
