Design System

Marigold System

A shared design language that let six product teams move faster without quietly drifting apart.

Role
Design Systems Lead
Timeline
Ongoing · 2022—
Scope
60+ components, 3 platforms

01 — Overview

Six teams, six slightly different buttons.

The company had grown from one product to a small suite, and each team had built its own UI as it went. Nothing was broken, exactly — but the products no longer felt related. There were eleven shades of “primary blue” in production and three different ideas of what a modal should do.

I was brought in to create Marigold: a system that could unify the surface without flattening the teams behind it.

02 — Problem

The hard part was never the components.

A shared library is easy to publish and easy to ignore. The real challenge was adoption: giving teams something so obviously better than rolling their own that the library became the path of least resistance, not a tax.

A design system isn’t a library. It’s an agreement.

So I treated Marigold as a product, with the engineers and designers on those six teams as its users. That meant research, a roadmap, release notes, and office hours — the unglamorous scaffolding that makes a system trustworthy enough to build on.

03 — Process

Tokens first, then components, then trust.

We started with an audit — every color, spacing value, and text style in production — and collapsed it into a single tier of design tokens. Components were rebuilt on top of those tokens so a change to the palette rippled everywhere at once, on purpose.

Governance was deliberately lightweight: a contribution path for new patterns, a clear “not yet” for one-offs, and documentation that explained the why, not just the props. People follow a system they understand.

04 — Outcome

From weeks of UI work to an afternoon.

Within two quarters, all six teams had adopted Marigold for new work. Teams reported that standing up a new screen went from days to hours, and the first full-product theme change — once a month-long project — shipped in a single afternoon.

More than the numbers, the products started to feel like a family again. Marigold is still evolving, and that’s the point: it’s infrastructure, not a deliverable.
